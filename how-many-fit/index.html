    How many buses long is the Titanic?

    It's one of those questions everyone has an instant answer to, and the answer is usually wrong. How Many Fit? asks you to picture one thing copied over and over, stacked up or lined up end to end, until it matches something much bigger. Then it draws the real answer to scale, copy by copy, so you can see exactly how far off you were.

    It's a different way of thinking about size. Instead of guessing metres, you think in "how many of these make one of those". That turns out to be how most people actually reason about big things.

    How to play How Many Fit?

    1. Read the puzzle. Heights are stacked, so it's "how many giraffes reach the top of the Eiffel Tower?". Lengths and widths are lined up, so it's "how many buses end to end match the Titanic?".
    2. Guess the count. Use the slider, tap − and +, or type a number. The two drawings are deliberately not to scale.
    3. Lock in and watch. The real number of copies appears next to the big thing at true scale, along with both measurements.
    4. Five rounds a day. Each is worth up to 200 points. Within 5% earns all 200, and four times too many or too few earns nothing.

    How scoring works

    What counts is how many times too many or too few you guessed, not the raw difference. Saying 30 when the answer is 22 is a decent guess; saying 3 when the answer is 2 is not.

    Tips

    • Use a halfway point. Twelve buses is too many to picture at once. Six, then double it, is much easier.
    • Remember what's measured. A tiger is measured with its tail and a peacock with its train, which makes them longer than you might think.
    • Tall things beat long things. Towers are easier to overestimate than ships. The eye exaggerates height.
    • Know a few sizes by heart. A person is about 1.7 m, a city bus 12 m, a giraffe 5.5 m, the Eiffel Tower 330 m. Divide one by the other and you're close.
    • Big counts hide small errors. On an answer of 60, guessing 55 still scores well. On an answer of 2, guessing 3 costs a lot, so think hardest on the small ones.

    About this game

    How Many Fit? uses the same library of real objects as Size It Up: animals, vehicles, landmarks, everyday things and even planets, each with a measured height, length or width from a published source. Each puzzle pairs a small thing with a much bigger one, so the answers range from about 2 to well over 100.

    The drawings on the puzzle are deliberately not to scale, so you can't just measure the screen. After you lock in, the real number of copies is drawn next to the big thing at true scale, which is often the moment people say "wait, really?" out loud.

    It's free, runs in your browser, works on phones and needs no account.

    Questions

    Is How Many Fit? free?

    Yes. The Daily and Unlimited are free, with no sign-up.

    Where do the sizes come from?

    From the same object library as How Big?, built from published references such as official landmark pages and manufacturer specifications.

    Can I play past puzzles?

    Yes. Every past Daily is in the archive.

    Why are the drawings on the puzzle not to scale?

    So the screen doesn't give the answer away. The true-scale picture appears as soon as you lock in your guess.

    What is a good score?

    550 or more makes you a Good Guesser, 750 a Sharp Eye and 900 a Scale Master. Five rounds are worth up to 1000.

    Can I play against my friends?

    Yes. Share your result as a challenge link, or start a Friends League so your group's Daily scores land on one table.
