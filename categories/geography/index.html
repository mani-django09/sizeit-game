About this category

Mountains are measured by height, waterfalls by their drop, rivers and lakes by length. This category puts them side by side so you can feel how a river like the Nile compares with a mountain, and how big a lake like Baikal really is.

Numbers come from standard geographic references, and every answer links to its source.

A few things worth knowing

  • Niagara Falls, on the US-Canada border, is about 51 m high. It is famous for how much water goes over it, not for its height.
  • Victoria Falls, between Zambia and Zimbabwe, is 108 m high but 1.7 km wide, which makes it one of the largest curtains of falling water on Earth.
  • Jog Falls in Karnataka drops 253 m in four streams, among the highest plunge waterfalls in India.

Questions

How does the geography Daily work?

You get five pairs from this category, the same for everyone that day. The grey object is drawn at its real size and you resize the orange one until the pair looks right. A green outline shows the truth, and each round is worth up to 200 points. A new set arrives at midnight, your time.

Where do the sizes come from?

From public references such as official pages and standard encyclopaedia figures. Every answer in the game links to its source after you lock in your guess.

Can I practise before the Daily?

Yes. Unlimited gives you fresh geography pairs as many times as you like, with no score pressure.
