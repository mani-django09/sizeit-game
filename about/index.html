About sizeit.games

Small daily games about how big the world really is.

Why we made this

Ask a room full of people how many buses long a blue whale is, and you'll hear "five", "ten", "twenty". The answer is about two. We are all surprisingly bad at scale: we know the Eiffel Tower is tall and an elephant is heavy, but put two things side by side and our sense of size falls apart.

sizeit.games turns that into a game. Each one takes two minutes, asks you to guess, and then shows the truth drawn to scale, with a short fact and a link to where the number came from. The guess is the fun part; the "wait, really?" afterwards is the part you remember.

What you can play

  • Eight Dailies, the same for everyone and new every midnight: Size It Up, Heavier or Lighter, Sort It, Ballpark, Speed Race, How Many Fit?, How Far? and Number Guess.
  • A Weekly Challenge with a new theme every Monday, and Survival, an endless mixed quiz against the clock.
  • Friends Leagues for your WhatsApp group, challenge links for any result, streaks, badges and an archive of every past Daily.

Where the numbers come from

The games share one library: over 90 real objects (animals, landmarks, vehicles, everyday things, planets), more than 70 number questions, nearly 80 cities and around 30 racers. Every size, weight and speed is checked against a public reference, such as an official landmark page, the Laws of Cricket, a manufacturer's specification or a well-known encyclopedia, and the source is linked after each fact in the games.

Animals and vehicles come in many sizes, so we use a typical adult or a standard model and say so. Distances between cities are never typed in: they are worked out from each city's latitude and longitude every time. Every drawing in the games is our own.

Spotted a number that looks wrong? Please tell us. We fix mistakes quickly, and new objects only join puzzles from the day they're added, so past Dailies never change.

What we care about

  • Quick. Two minutes a day is enough. No endless feeds, no ads between rounds.
  • Fair. Everyone gets the same Daily, so comparing scores with friends means something.
  • Private. No accounts, no emails, no tracking cookies. Your scores live on your device. The privacy policy lists the little that is sent.
  • For everyone. Metres or feet, kilometres or miles, phone or laptop. The games work offline once you've visited, and you can add the site to your home screen.

Who we are

sizeit.games is a small, independent project made for players everywhere, with a soft spot for Indian landmarks, cricket and auto rickshaws. There's no big company behind it, just people who like a good "how big is that, really?" argument.

Ideas, corrections and kind words are always welcome on the contact page, and you can suggest an object for a future Daily.
