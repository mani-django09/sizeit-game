Close enough is the whole point

Nobody remembers that the Earth is 12,742 km across, or that a blue whale weighs about 150 tonnes. But plenty of people could tell you the Earth is "thousands of kilometres" across and a whale weighs "a hundred-odd tonnes". That sense of the right ballpark is what Ballpark tests.

Every question has a real number for an answer, but you never type it. You just pick the range it falls in: single digits, tens, hundreds, thousands, and all the way up to the billions and beyond. It's quick, it's forgiving, and it's surprisingly hard to get ten in a row.

How to play Ballpark

  1. Read the question. It might be about space, the human body, animals, landmarks, sport or everyday maths, and it always says which unit to think in.
  2. Pick a range. Five buttons show neighbouring ranges, each ten times bigger than the last, such as 10–99, 100–999 and 1,000–9,999. Tap one, or press 1 to 5.
  3. See the real number. The right range lights up green, along with the exact answer and a short fact.
  4. Score. The exact range earns 3 points and the range next to it earns 1. Ten questions a day, 30 points in total.

Daily or Rush

Daily

Ten questions, the same for everyone, with a new set at midnight. Take your time, then share your score as a row of coloured squares.

60-second Rush

As many as you can in a minute. Every exact range scores a point, and every miss takes three seconds off the clock. Your best score is saved.

Tips

  • Count the zeros. Every range is one more digit than the one before it. "Is it a three-digit number or a four-digit number?" is the only question you need to answer.
  • Mind the unit. A distance in kilometres and the same distance in metres are three ranges apart. Read the unit before you tap.
  • Do a little maths. Seconds in a day? 24 × 60 × 60 is "about a hundred thousand" before you've even finished multiplying.
  • When torn, go bigger. People almost always underestimate huge numbers: distances in space, the age of the Earth, the weight of buildings.

Questions

Is Ballpark free?

Yes. The Daily and the Rush are both free, with no account and nothing to download.

What does "10k–99k" mean?

Ten thousand up to just under a hundred thousand. Above that, M means million, B means billion and T means trillion. Ranges beyond that are written as powers of ten.

Where do the questions come from?

From the same well-sourced facts used in Number Guess, plus the sizes and weights from the object library behind How Big? and Heavier or Lighter.

When is the next Daily?

At midnight your local time. Everyone gets the same ten questions that day.

Can I challenge a friend?

Yes. Share any result and the link becomes a challenge. A Rush link even gives your friend the same questions in the same order. For your whole group, start a Friends League.

Can I play past Dailies?

Yes. Every past Ballpark Daily is in the archive.
