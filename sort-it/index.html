    A daily game about putting things in order

    Comparing two things is easy. Ranking five is where it gets interesting. Is a king cobra longer than a Bengal tiger? Does a giraffe outweigh a hatchback? Where does an ostrich fit between a scooter and an auto rickshaw? Sort It hands you five real-world things and asks you to line them up, and then it shows them standing side by side so you can see exactly where you went wrong.

    Every day brings two rounds: one sorted by size and one sorted by weight. The same object can land in a very different place in each, which is half the fun.

    How to play Sort It

    1. Read the round. The first round asks you to sort by size. Each card says what is being measured: height for a tower or a giraffe, length for a snake or a ship, width for a coin or a ball. The second sorts by weight.
    2. Tap in order. Tap the smallest (or lightest) item first, then the next, until all five slots are filled. Changed your mind? Tap a placed item to send it back, or start over.
    3. Check your order. The size round lines everything up at true scale, and the weight round shows every weight, so you can see the real order at a glance.
    4. Score up to 10 per round. You get a point for every pair you put the right way round. There are ten pairs among five items, so a perfect order scores 10, and a near miss still scores well.

    Why pairs, not positions?

    Imagine you get everything right except the first item, which you put last. Scoring by position would give you zero, even though you understood almost everything. Counting pairs is fairer. That mistake only breaks the pairs involving that one item, so you'd still score 6 out of 10. The closer your order is to the truth, the more points you keep.

    Same five things, two different orders

    Here's why the weight round is never just the size round again. Take five animals from the game library:

    By size (smallest first)By weight (lightest first)
    House cat, 0.75 m with its tailHouse cat, about 4.5 kg
    Peacock, 2.1 m with its trainPeacock, about 5 kg
    Ostrich, 2.5 m tallKing cobra, about 6 kg
    Bengal tiger, 2.9 m with its tailOstrich, about 110 kg
    King cobra, 4 m longBengal tiger, about 220 kg

    The cobra goes from the biggest to the middle, because long and thin is light. That kind of swap is exactly what Sort It is built to catch.

    Tips

    • Find the extremes first. The smallest and biggest are usually obvious. Lock them in and you've already settled seven of the ten pairs.
    • Size isn't weight. A peacock's train makes it longer than a house cat by a long way, but they weigh almost the same. Long and light is common in nature.
    • Read the small label. In the size round, a snake is measured nose to tail and a tower from base to tip. Compare the measurements, not how big the drawings look on the cards.

    Questions

    Is Sort It free?

    Yes. Play the Daily and as many Unlimited rounds as you like, with no account needed.

    When is the next Daily?

    At midnight your local time. Everyone gets the same two rounds on the same day.

    Where do the sizes and weights come from?

    From the same object library as the other games on this site, built from published references. Animals and vehicles use typical values.

    Can I challenge a friend?

    Yes. Share any result and your friend plays the same rounds with your score to beat. For a group, start a Friends League.

    Can I play past Dailies?

    Yes. Every past Sort It Daily is in the archive.
