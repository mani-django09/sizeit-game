    Size it up: how big is it, really?

    Quick question. A blue whale or a city bus: how many buses long is the whale? Most people say four or five. The real answer is about two. That gap between what we picture and what is actually out there is the whole idea behind How Big?, a free daily game where you size it up by eye and then see the truth drawn to scale.

    Every round puts two things side by side. One is already at its real size. The other is yours to stretch or shrink until the pair looks right. Lock it in, and a green outline shows where it should have been. Some rounds you'll nail. Others will make you laugh at how far off you were, and that's usually the round you remember the next day.

    How to play Size It Up

    1. Look at the grey object. It's drawn at its true size, so treat it as your ruler. The label tells you whether you're comparing height, length or diameter.
    2. Resize the orange one. Drag left or right on the board, move the slider, tap the − and + buttons, or use the arrow keys. Hold Shift with an arrow key for bigger jumps. On a laptop, the scroll wheel works too.
    3. Press Lock in (or hit Enter) when it looks right. There's no timer, so take a second look if you want.
    4. See the real size. A green outline appears at the true size, along with both numbers, your points and a short fact about the object.
    5. Play all five rounds. Each is worth up to 200 points, so a perfect day is 1000. Then share your score, which shows coloured squares and no spoilers, or send a friend the same set.

    How scoring works

    Points depend on how many times too big or too small your guess was, not on how many metres you missed by. Being 2 metres off on a giraffe is a big miss. Being 2 metres off on the Burj Khalifa is basically perfect. Because the score uses ratios, a tall tower and a tiny coin are scored the same way.

    Tips for sizing things up

    • Start from something you know. A person is about 1.7 m, a door about 2.1 m, a car about 4 m. Picture that inside the scene before you start dragging.
    • Think in multiples, not metres. Ask "is it twice as tall, or ten times?" first. Get that roughly right and you'll keep most of your points.
    • Check the measurement. A cobra is measured nose to tail, a coin across its face, a statue from foot to top. Read the label so you're not comparing a length with a height.
    • Be wary of famous things. Fame makes objects feel bigger. The Mona Lisa is only 77 cm tall, a little shorter than a kitchen counter.
    • Big things are bigger than you think. The Titanic was about four jumbo jets long. The Burj Khalifa is roughly two and a half Eiffel Towers. When something is huge, people tend to guess too small.

    About this game

    How Big? started with a simple, slightly embarrassing discovery: most of us are terrible at scale. We know the Eiffel Tower is tall and an ostrich is big for a bird, but put them next to something else and our sense of size falls apart. A game turned out to be the most enjoyable way to fix that, a few minutes at a time.

    There are around forty real-world objects in the library right now, from a ₹10 coin and a cricket ball to the Statue of Unity, Qutub Minar, the Great Pyramid and a Boeing 747. Every drawing is original and every size comes from a public reference, such as official landmark pages, the Laws of Cricket and manufacturer specifications. Animals and vehicles come in many sizes, so for those we use a typical adult or a standard model and say so in the fact.

    The game runs in your browser with no account, and no download. It works on phones, tablets and desktops, switches between metric and imperial with one tap, and can be added to your home screen so it plays offline. Your scores and streaks stay on your own device.

    Questions people ask

    Is Size It Up free to play?

    Yes. Every mode, including Daily, Unlimited and challenges, is free. There's nothing to sign up for.

    Who makes this game?

    It's made by sizeit.games, an independent site, with its own artwork, objects and scoring. Add it to your morning along with Heavier or Lighter, Sort It and Ballpark on the same site.

    When does the daily puzzle reset?

    At midnight in your local time. Everyone gets the same five pairs on the same date, so you can compare scores fairly.

    What counts as a good score?

    Your result comes with a title. 550 or more makes you a Good Guesser, 750 a Sharp Eye, and 900 a Scale Master. A full 1000 means every guess was within 5%, which is rare enough to be worth a screenshot.

    How do I keep my streak?

    Finish the Daily once a day. The flame counter at the top shows your current streak, and the stats panel keeps your best streak, average and high score.

    Can I play on my phone?

    Yes. Drag with your finger on the board or use the slider. Add the page to your home screen and it opens like an app.
